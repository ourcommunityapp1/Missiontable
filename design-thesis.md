# Mission Table — Design Thesis

*A framework for how we look, feel, and mean.*

---

## The Central Question

Every design decision we make should answer one question: **does this honor the people at the table?**

Both the people gathering in an American living room — and the people living in the countries those tables are praying for.

---

## Five Themes

### 1. Every Soul Has a Face

We are a travel site that goes somewhere deeper than tourism. The nations we feature are not destinations — they are peoples. Photography, type, and copy must reflect this. We do not use imagery that exoticizes, flattens, or aestheticizes poverty. We show the full humanity of every culture: its beauty, its ordinariness, its weight. Our visual hierarchy always moves toward the person, not the statistic.

### 2. The Table Is the Motif

Not a logo. Not an icon. The table is the design system's load-bearing structure — the one object that appears in every culture, every faith tradition, every era of human life. It is domestic and cosmic at the same time. Every layout and composition should have a sense of gathering: things drawn toward a center, an implied warmth, a place where people belong.

### 3. Raw Over Refined

We are not a luxury brand, a tech startup, or a polished nonprofit. Mission Table should feel like it was built by someone who believed this enough to make it with their own hands. That means: honest materials, unadorned type, real photography over illustration, function over cleverness. If a design choice exists to impress, cut it. If it exists to serve, keep it.

### 4. Proclamation Typography

Type is our boldest act. The oversized country wordmarks — SPAIN. MOROCCO. MYANMAR. — are not decorative; they are declarations. These nations are known. They are named. They are prayed for. Our typographic system leans into scale, weight, and contrast as acts of proclamation, not self-expression. Type should feel like it belongs on a wall, not a business card.

### 5. Color Borrowed, Not Owned

We do not have brand colors in the traditional sense. Color comes from the nations — borrowed from their flags, their landscapes, their textiles, their kitchens. This is an act of hospitality: we take our cues from the people we're praying for. The base palette is neutral and warm (ready to receive color from any country), and the accent is always sourced from the specific culture being featured.

---

## What We Reject

**Trendy.** If it's on every landing page this year, it's not for us.

**Upscale.** Premium finishes signal exclusivity. We are for every table.

**Sentimentality.** The Great Commission is not soft. Neither is our design.

**Spectacle.** We resist the urge to make the site about how the site looks.

---

## The Guiding Sentence

*Bold enough to declare something true. Simple enough for any table.*
